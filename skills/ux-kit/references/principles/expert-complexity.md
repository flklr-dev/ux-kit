**Explanation:** Efficient expert use can require visible information and shortcuts; reducing item counts is not an outcome by itself.

**Useful when:** Tables, dispatch tools, dashboards, search and filtering.

**Signals:** Arbitrary seven-item limits replace observed task needs; hiding data forces remembering or repeated navigation.

**Interventions:** Use the audience, comparison task, observations and configurable views to decide what stays visible.

**Limits:** Expert familiarity does not excuse access barriers. A small successful study supports its tested tasks, not every future audience.

**Example:** Keep nine comparison columns when trained operators demonstrably use all nine.

**Validation:** Compare task completion, errors and workload with representative novices or experts as applicable.

**Evidence boundary:** Expert-efficiency heuristic inspected; the number of useful columns depends on observed task evidence, not a universal threshold.

**Counterexample:** An expert table with inaccessible controls still needs repair even if its density is useful.

**Conflicts:** Preserve comparison and shortcuts while satisfying access and new-user needs through appropriate alternatives.
