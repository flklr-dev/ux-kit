**Explanation:** An apparently overwhelming decision can reflect unfamiliar attributes, unclear preferences or consequences. A large set alone does not identify the problem.

**Signals:** Customers repeatedly compare plans without knowing which attributes matter for their task.

**Interventions:** Offer honest comparison aids and optional narrowing based on stated needs; keep the full set accessible.

**Limits:** Do not assert that more choices always lower satisfaction or completion; original studies were not inspected.

**Example:** A hosting picker explains region and retention consequences before offering a reversible recommendation.

**Validation:** Compare decision confidence with subsequent fit and correction, not conversion alone.

**Counterexample:** Hiding specialist plans blocks users who already know the exact configuration they need.

**Conflicts:** Resolve narrowing against autonomy and expert access; distinguish decision support from forced funneling.

**Evidence boundary:** Qualified account of a popular effect; no empirical choice-overload claim or universal optimal set size is made.

**Useful when:** Investigate overwhelming unfamiliar options without deleting useful expert choices.
