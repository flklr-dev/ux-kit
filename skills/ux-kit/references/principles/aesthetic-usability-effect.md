**Explanation:** A favorable impression and demonstrated task success are different measurements.

**Signals:** A team calls a new form usable because one participant liked its colors.

**Interventions:** Preserve a coherent visual system, then observe completion, error correction and recovery separately from preference.

**Limits:** Attractiveness does not prove access, correctness or ease of completing a particular task.

**Example:** A polished report editor is tested through failed submission before its usability claim is accepted.

**Validation:** Record visual preference alongside actual task success and barriers, keeping the results distinct.

**Counterexample:** An attractive dialog that traps keyboard users is not validated by positive ratings.

**Conflicts:** Resolve aesthetic polish against operational evidence and access requirements.

**Evidence boundary:** Popular perceived-usability account inspected; original experiments not reviewed and no causal performance benefit asserted.

**Useful when:** Separate favorable first impressions from observed task success.
