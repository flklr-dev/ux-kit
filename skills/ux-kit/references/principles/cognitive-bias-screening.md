**Explanation:** Use a bias label to form a testable question about a particular decision, not to diagnose people or explain every mistake after the fact.

**Signals:** A team claims customers are biased because they reject its recommended plan.

**Interventions:** Identify the presented evidence, alternative explanations and observable decision error; compare neutral presentations.

**Limits:** A named bias is not proof of causation, irrationality or a need for persuasion.

**Example:** A default AI suggestion is tested with a plausible planted error to see whether reviewers catch it.

**Validation:** Record error detection, corrections and reasons; distinguish framing effects from missing information.

**Counterexample:** Calling an informed preference irrational can hide a legitimate budget constraint.

**Conflicts:** Prefer user agency and evidence over an intervention designed solely to force acceptance.

**Evidence boundary:** The inspected synthesis introduces bias concepts; this screening method is original practice and does not diagnose a cognitive mechanism.

**Useful when:** Identify a specific judgment risk instead of diagnosing users from a bias label.
