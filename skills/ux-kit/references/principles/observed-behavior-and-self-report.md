**Explanation:** Stated preference, remembered experience and observed task behavior answer different questions.

**Signals:** Positive comments are presented as proof of faster or error-free completion.

**Interventions:** Record what was said separately from what was observed, then investigate discrepancies without dismissing either.

**Limits:** Self-report is valuable for reasons and experience; observation can also be distorted by task/context.

**Example:** A participant likes an invoice layout but cannot locate its due date; both findings remain in the report.

**Validation:** Compare recorded statements, task actions and outcome under the same evidence boundary.

**Counterexample:** Ignoring a participant's privacy concern because they completed the task loses relevant evidence.

**Conflicts:** Use the method matching the claim rather than treating one evidence type as universally superior.

**Evidence boundary:** Official interview/observation-analysis guidance inspected; distinctions are a qualified evaluation practice, not a measured bias diagnosis.

**Useful when:** Keep stated preference distinct from demonstrated task performance.
