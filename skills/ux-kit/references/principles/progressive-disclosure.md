**Explanation:** Choose what appears initially using task frequency and dependency; progressively disclosed options should remain easy to find.

**Signals:** Frequent controls are hidden behind Advanced, or rare technical settings dominate the initial form.

**Interventions:** Keep common needs visible, expose secondary options clearly and preserve their values when collapsed.

**Limits:** Do not hide necessary comparisons or equate a wizard with progressive disclosure.

**Example:** A print setup shows printer and copies immediately, with rare output adjustments in a clearly labeled section.

**Validation:** Test common and specialist tasks, including discovering, changing and returning from advanced settings.

**Counterexample:** Splitting interdependent booking price/date choices across steps makes comparison harder.

**Conflicts:** Preserve expert complexity and cross-option dependencies; use a staged flow only when the task supports it.

**Evidence boundary:** First-party guidance and limitations inspected; the initial/secondary split must be verified for this product.

**Useful when:** Reveal advanced settings without hiding frequently required controls.
