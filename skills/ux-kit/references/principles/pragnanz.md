**Explanation:** Treat simpler visual organization as a candidate interpretation aid, not permission to simplify the user's task away.

**Signals:** Competing outlines and decorative diagonals obscure a table's row structure.

**Interventions:** Remove misleading decoration and clarify consistent alignment while preserving needed comparisons.

**Limits:** No universal simplest arrangement or deletion quota follows.

**Example:** A dispatch table keeps its eight useful fields but removes ornamental panels that suggest false subgroups.

**Validation:** Observe row comparison and mistaken associations before and after the layout change.

**Counterexample:** Replacing the table with minimal cards hides relationships users must compare.

**Conflicts:** Task completeness and accessibility take precedence over an abstract simplicity preference.

**Evidence boundary:** Popular formulation inspected; original perceptual research was not verified and no universal perceptual outcome is asserted.

**Useful when:** Reduce ambiguous visual organization without removing required information.
