**Explanation:** Miller's paper treats different information-processing tasks and recoding. It does not prescribe seven destinations, cards or columns.

**Signals:** A redesign cites seven as the reason to remove working options.

**Interventions:** Identify whether the task involves recall, recognition or expert lookup; judge the actual retrieval difficulty.

**Limits:** Historical task-specific findings do not establish current product performance.

**Example:** A searchable list of forty depot codes stays intact because dispatchers recognize and retrieve codes successfully.

**Validation:** Compare lookup success and errors for real destinations; do not score compliance with seven.

**Counterexample:** Removing required comparison columns to reach seven makes the job harder.

**Conflicts:** Recognition and expertise can outweigh a simplistic memory interpretation.

**Evidence boundary:** Original reprint inspected; absolute judgment and immediate memory differ. No universal menu limit or modern capacity estimate is asserted.

**Useful when:** Assess memory demands without imposing a seven-item menu limit.
