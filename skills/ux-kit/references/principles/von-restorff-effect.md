**Explanation:** A distinctive item can invite attention, but salience and later recall are different outcomes that need checking.

**Signals:** Every alert, badge and primary action uses the same strong emphasis.

**Interventions:** Reserve a clear accessible distinction for the genuinely consequential item; reduce competing decoration.

**Limits:** No guaranteed recall, comprehension or action follows from visual difference.

**Example:** A review table distinguishes the one unresolved conflict with text and an icon while ordinary rows stay calm.

**Validation:** Ask users to locate and correctly explain the conflict; verify color-independent access.

**Counterexample:** Making a discounted plan distinctive can distract from its recurring cost.

**Conflicts:** Resolve salience against hierarchy, honest comparison and accessibility.

**Evidence boundary:** Editorial distinctiveness account inspected; original experiment and product effect not verified.

**Useful when:** Distinguish a genuinely important item without making every item salient.
