**Explanation:** Relative emphasis should help locate the decision and the information needed to make it.

**Signals:** A promotional badge dominates while the final total and payment action are hard to locate.

**Interventions:** Prioritize the real total, conditions and primary action through consistent type, grouping and contrast.

**Limits:** Larger or louder is not automatically clearer; preserve supporting terms and accessible order.

**Example:** A checkout places the payable total and charge timing above the commitment action, with promotion secondary.

**Validation:** Ask users what they will pay and when; inspect reading and focus order at narrow widths.

**Counterexample:** An enormous purchase button with tiny conditions increases prominence while weakening comprehension.

**Conflicts:** Balance action visibility with informed commitment and useful detail.

**Evidence boundary:** NN/g supplies visual-design guidance; the checkout composition is an original application with no conversion claim.

**Useful when:** Prioritize decisions, supporting details and secondary actions.
