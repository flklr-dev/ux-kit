**Explanation:** Consequential product claims should have a meaningful basis the person can inspect.

**Signals:** A recommendation appears independent but its ranking basis is undisclosed or unsupported.

**Interventions:** Explain the relevant basis and limitations; provide authentic evidence and distinguish promotion from comparison.

**Limits:** Do not manufacture endorsements, certification or evidence simply to fill a trust section.

**Example:** An equipment recommendation names compatible models from an actual maintained source and exposes its update limits.

**Validation:** Trace a sample claim to its real evidence and check that the wording matches its scope.

**Counterexample:** Adding an unsupported trust badge can increase apparent credibility while reducing truthfulness.

**Conflicts:** Trust presentation must follow evidence, not substitute for it.

**Evidence boundary:** Staff report identifies misleading evidence/advertising patterns; this verifiability method is an original task application.

**Useful when:** Support consequential claims with inspectable evidence.
