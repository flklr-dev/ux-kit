**Explanation:** Controlled choice-response models raise questions about decision structure. Distinguish response selection from reading, searching, preference formation and learned lookup.

**Signals:** Unfamiliar plans differ on hidden dimensions and customers cannot explain the tradeoffs.

**Interventions:** Clarify comparable attributes and useful groups; test decision success before reducing options.

**Limits:** No universal response-time prediction applies to arbitrary menus; the original full paper and Hyman study were not inspected.

**Example:** Plan selection presents the same storage and support dimensions for each plan, keeping specialist options reachable.

**Validation:** Measure correct fit, comprehension and completion time for this audience rather than option count alone.

**Counterexample:** A known-item search among forty familiar codes may outperform six vague categories.

**Conflicts:** Preserve expert retrieval and meaningful choice while reducing avoidable interpretation.

**Evidence boundary:** Publisher abstract inspected for the limited experimental framing; modern product application remains qualified, with no fitted formula or guaranteed fewer-is-faster claim.

**Useful when:** Structure a genuinely difficult plan-selection decision.
