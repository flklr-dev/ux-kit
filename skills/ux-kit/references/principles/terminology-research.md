**Explanation:** Investigate which words people actually use and what they mean before choosing labels.

**Signals:** Support/search terms repeatedly differ from the product's labels.

**Interventions:** Review real questions and queries, ask for meaning in task context and test candidate labels without leading participants.

**Limits:** Frequency alone does not establish meaning; do not assume the loudest segment represents everyone.

**Example:** A help label is tested against customers asking about ending renewal rather than internal account lifecycle jargon.

**Validation:** Record terms, intended tasks and observed destination choice across relevant audiences.

**Counterexample:** A popular shorthand can be ambiguous for first-time users or another locale.

**Conflicts:** Balance established expert vocabulary with discoverability and explain genuine semantic differences.

**Evidence boundary:** Reader-focused guidance informs this original terminology method; no vocabulary match is asserted without actual research.

**Useful when:** Check whether product terms match user vocabulary.
