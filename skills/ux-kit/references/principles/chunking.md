**Explanation:** Meaningful groups depend on learned relationships. Grouping can help structure a task without making every group a fixed size.

**Signals:** One undifferentiated account form mixes billing identity, delivery and notification settings.

**Interventions:** Use headings based on task concepts; keep dependent fields together and retain useful overview.

**Limits:** Arbitrary equal-sized cards are not meaningful chunks; audience knowledge matters.

**Example:** A supplier form groups billing address separately from delivery instructions, allowing each to be checked as a unit.

**Validation:** Test whether people locate and correct fields without opening unrelated groups.

**Counterexample:** Splitting an address into tiny screens prevents checking whether its parts agree.

**Conflicts:** Balance grouped sections against cross-section comparisons; keep summary context where needed.

**Evidence boundary:** Miller's reprint discusses learned recoding in specific memory tasks. Form grouping is an extrapolation requiring verification, not evidence for a fixed group size.

**Useful when:** Group account details into meaningful sections.
