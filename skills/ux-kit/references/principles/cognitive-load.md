**Explanation:** A task can demand reasoning about its subject and avoidable effort interpreting the interface. Inspect where effort comes from before simplifying.

**Signals:** People repeatedly reopen instructions or translate unexplained setting names.

**Interventions:** Keep the current goal, relevant values and consequences visible; remove irrelevant interruptions.

**Limits:** A hard professional task may remain complex; counting controls does not measure mental effort.

**Example:** A backup setup shows retention meaning beside the choice, so users need not remember a separate policy page.

**Validation:** Observe setup choices, backtracking and explanations with representative new and experienced users.

**Counterexample:** A one-control wizard hides interacting price and retention choices, increasing comparison effort.

**Conflicts:** Resolve simplicity against expert comparison evidence, not visual sparseness.

**Evidence boundary:** The inspected editorial framework supports a design hypothesis; no workload reduction or universal capacity was measured here.

**Useful when:** Keep a setup task understandable without remembering hidden values.
