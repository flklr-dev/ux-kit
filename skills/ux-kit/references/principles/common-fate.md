**Explanation:** Coordinated change can imply that elements belong together. Use motion only when the relationship is real and offer stable equivalents.

**Signals:** Independent uploads animate as a single unit despite different outcomes.

**Interventions:** Keep per-item status legible and motion optional; coordinate only genuinely linked changes.

**Limits:** Perception research does not establish that an animation communicates completion or is safe for every user.

**Example:** A batch shows separate completed and failed items instead of moving every item into a success group.

**Validation:** Test mixed outcomes with motion reduced and disabled; inspect item-state understanding.

**Counterexample:** Animating all rows together implies every row saved after only some succeeded.

**Conflicts:** Truthful status and reduced-motion access override coordinated presentation.

**Evidence boundary:** Review of common-fate research inspected; no measured motion benefit or universal grouping response is claimed.

**Useful when:** Use coordinated motion to communicate a genuine relationship.
