**Explanation:** Ordering may matter when a task genuinely requires later recall. Inspect the memory task before applying a serial-position explanation.

**Signals:** A tour expects users to recall a long sequence after it disappears.

**Interventions:** Keep necessary steps accessible during work; investigate ordering with a realistic delayed task.

**Limits:** Do not reorder navigation, pricing or safety information solely to exploit a named memory effect.

**Example:** A training checklist stays available on return, so the final step does not depend on recalling a vanished slide.

**Validation:** Check delayed task completion and access to instructions, rather than recall of a law name.

**Counterexample:** A frequently consulted alphabetical reference benefits from stable lookup order rather than an arbitrary memorable beginning.

**Conflicts:** Findability and visible recognition may remove the recall burden more effectively than reordering.

**Evidence boundary:** Popular effect account inspected; original recall experiments and applicability to this product remain unverified.

**Useful when:** Investigate ordering where remembering a sequence actually matters.
