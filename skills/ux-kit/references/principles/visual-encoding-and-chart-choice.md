**Explanation:** Choose the visual encoding for the actual relationship people need to judge and provide useful access to the information.

**Signals:** A decorative chart obscures a simple comparison or implies continuous change across unrelated categories.

**Interventions:** Define the question, try suitable representations and verify labels/scales and an accessible alternative.

**Limits:** A chart-type lookup does not validate the chart or establish that every baseline must be identical across encodings.

**Example:** A monthly trend uses ordered time values while an exact invoice comparison retains a table.

**Validation:** Ask representative readers to make the intended comparison; inspect scale/label truth and equivalent data access.

**Counterexample:** A visually striking area shape can imply a magnitude difference unsupported by its numbers.

**Conflicts:** Balance rapid overview with exact comparison and nonvisual access.

**Evidence boundary:** Official relationship/chart-choice guidance inspected; no universal best chart or measured comprehension claim is made.

**Useful when:** Choose encodings suited to the comparison task.
