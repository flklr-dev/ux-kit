**Explanation:** Combine complementary evidence to investigate a question, checking whether apparent agreement comes from the same underlying source.

**Signals:** A click log, dashboard and report all repeat one event and are counted as three independent confirmations.

**Interventions:** Choose methods exposing different uncertainties, retain provenance and investigate contradictions.

**Limits:** More sources do not automatically mean stronger evidence; common bias or correlated data can persist.

**Example:** A support spike motivates observing the failing task and inspecting its trace, rather than simply averaging three summaries of the same tickets.

**Validation:** Trace each conclusion to independent observations and record unresolved disagreement and coverage gaps.

**Counterexample:** A replicated screenshot annotation is not new runtime evidence.

**Conflicts:** Match evidence strength to consequence and keep method limits visible.

**Evidence boundary:** Official question/method and observation guidance informs this original triangulation practice; no formal statistical independence or causal proof inferred.

**Useful when:** Use complementary evidence to investigate a consequential uncertainty.
