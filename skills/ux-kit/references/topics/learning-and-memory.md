# Learning And Memory

Distinguish teaching an unfamiliar skill from helping someone complete the current task. Keep instructions available, provide appropriate low-cost exploration and test later use. Recall effects are qualified questions, not a reason to hide instructions or pressure unfinished tasks. Jakob and transfer applications resolve to the consistency entry in the usability topic.

When specifying training or onboarding for an unfamiliar workflow, make these deliverables explicit:

| Deliverable | Concrete specification |
|---|---|
| Practice | Initial example, later independent attempts, explanatory feedback and safe recovery; exact intervals are hypotheses tied to shifts/opportunities. |
| Help during ordinary work | A task-specific entry point and useful help content available while doing the live task, without entering practice or losing the current draft. Keep this distinct from capability-building practice. |
| Capacity and re-entry | Total scheduled time plus a stop/resume rule for errors, retries, fewer shifts and extra practice. Keep ordinary work available, retain progress and let familiar users bypass known material while reaching unfamiliar tasks. Preserve working controls and shortcuts. |
| Delayed assessment | Later realistic tasks with independent completion/help-use observations. Record intervening task exposure, comprehension and domain knowledge; immediate quizzes repeating tutorial wording do not establish durable capability. |

Capacity is a limit on actual practice time, including explanations, errors and retries, not only the nominal activity schedule. Specify `Time used | Stop condition | Saved progress | Next opportunity | Remaining total allowance`. For example, if a protected session ends during a retry, stop at a safe checkpoint, retain the attempt and feedback, and offer resumption within the remaining agreed allowance; if none remains, defer optional practice and keep ordinary work/help available. Do not add a full retry on top of an exhausted session or silently restart its timer. Exact session lengths and scheduling remain supplied constraints or hypotheses, not optimal-learning claims.

- [Serial Position Effect](../principles/serial-position-effect.md): Investigate ordering where remembering a sequence actually matters.
- [Von Restorff Effect](../principles/von-restorff-effect.md): Distinguish a genuinely important item without making every item salient.
- [Zeigarnik Effect](../principles/zeigarnik-effect.md): Treat unfinished-task reminders as a hypothesis rather than a coercive device.
- [Spacing Effect](../principles/spacing-effect.md): Plan repeated learning opportunities for an unfamiliar workflow.
- [Progressive Disclosure](../principles/progressive-disclosure.md): Reveal advanced settings without hiding frequently required controls.
- [Progressive Onboarding](../principles/progressive-onboarding.md): Introduce guidance when a feature becomes relevant.
- [Error-Tolerant Exploration](../principles/error-tolerant-exploration.md): Let users learn an editor through low-cost reversible actions.
- [Contextual Help](../principles/contextual-help.md): Place useful assistance beside the task requiring it.

Preserve successful behavior. Prefer scoped accessibility and platform requirements over a conflicting heuristic; report evidence limits.
