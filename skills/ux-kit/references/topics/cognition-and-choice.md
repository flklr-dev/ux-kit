# Cognition And Choice

First ask whether the difficulty is remembering hidden facts, interpreting unfamiliar alternatives, finding a known item or forming a preference. Preserve working expert lookup. Miller and Hick are not option-count quotas; chunking changes meaningful organization, while recognition and visible context reduce recall demands.

- [Cognitive Load](../principles/cognitive-load.md): Keep a setup task understandable without remembering hidden values.
- [Working Memory](../principles/working-memory.md): Keep comparison information visible while users make a decision.
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md): Group account details into meaningful sections.
- [Miller’s Law](../principles/millers-law.md): Assess memory demands without imposing a seven-item menu limit.
- [Mental Models](../principles/mental-models.md): Make a scheduling feature match users’ expectations of time and ownership.
- [Hick–Hyman Law](../principles/hick-hyman-law.md): Structure a genuinely difficult plan-selection decision.
- [Choice Overload](../principles/choice-overload.md): Investigate overwhelming unfamiliar options without deleting useful expert choices.
- [Recognition Rather Than Recall](../principles/recognition-rather-than-recall.md): Show available actions and previously selected values.
- [Selective Attention](../principles/selective-attention.md): Make task-relevant changes detectable without flooding the screen.
- [Cognitive Bias Screening](../principles/cognitive-bias-screening.md): Identify a specific judgment risk instead of diagnosing users from a bias label.

Preserve successful behavior. Prefer scoped accessibility and platform requirements over a conflicting heuristic; report evidence limits.
